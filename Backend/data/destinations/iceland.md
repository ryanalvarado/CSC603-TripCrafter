# Iceland

Reykjavik is the capital and main base for travelers. The Golden Circle day trip (Þingvellir, Geysir, Gullfoss) is the most popular route and doable in one day. The Ring Road (Route 1) circles the entire island in about 1,300 km — most travelers need 7-10 days. The South Coast (Vík, Jökulsárlón glacier lagoon, black sand beaches) is the most accessible region.

Car rental is essential outside Reykjavik. A 2WD is fine for the Ring Road in summer; 4WD/AWD is required for highland F-roads and winter driving. Gas stations are spaced far apart — fill up whenever you can. Reykjavik has a limited bus system (Strætó) but no trains or metro.

Iceland is cashless — cards are accepted everywhere, even at remote hot springs. Tipping is not practiced. Tap water is excellent and safe to drink (it comes from glacial springs). Bring a reusable bottle.

Summer (June-August) offers midnight sun with nearly 24 hours of daylight. Winter (November-February) has only 4-5 hours of daylight but is the best time for Northern Lights. Weather changes rapidly year-round — dress in waterproof layers. Wind is constant and strong.

Iceland is expensive. A basic restaurant meal costs ISK 3,000-5,000 (about $20-35). Gas is about $2.50/liter. Budget travelers rely on grocery stores (Bónus, Krónan) and cooking at guesthouses. Hot dogs from Bæjarins Beztu are an iconic budget meal at around $4.
