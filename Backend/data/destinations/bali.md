# Bali, Indonesia

Bali's main tourist areas are Seminyak (upscale beach clubs and dining), Ubud (cultural heart with rice terraces and temples), Canggu (surfing and digital nomads), and Nusa Dua (luxury resorts). Kuta is busy and touristy — most travelers prefer Seminyak or Canggu instead.

Transportation relies heavily on scooter rentals (about IDR 70,000/day) and ride-hailing apps like Grab and Gojek. Official taxis (Blue Bird) are metered and reliable. Traffic congestion is severe, especially between Seminyak and Ubud — allow 1.5-2 hours for that trip.

Temple visits require a sarong and sash covering your legs. Many temples provide rentals for a small donation. Offerings (canang sari) are placed everywhere on the ground — be careful not to step on them. The left hand is considered unclean; use your right hand when giving or receiving items.

Dry season (April-October) is the best time to visit. Wet season (November-March) brings heavy afternoon rain but lower prices and fewer tourists. Nyepi (Day of Silence, usually in March) shuts down the entire island for 24 hours — no flights, no leaving your hotel.

A meal at a local warung costs IDR 25,000-50,000 (about $1.50-3). Western restaurants in Seminyak charge $8-20 per meal. Bali's water is not safe to drink from the tap.
