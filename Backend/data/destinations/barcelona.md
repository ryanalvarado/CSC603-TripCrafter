# Barcelona, Spain

Barcelona's main areas are the Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic), Las Ramblas, Eixample (where most Gaudí buildings are), Barceloneta (beach), and Gràcia (local, bohemian). El Born is excellent for restaurants and boutiques. Avoid pickpockets on Las Ramblas and the metro.

The TMB metro has 12 lines running 5 AM to midnight (24 hours on Saturdays). A T-casual card gives 10 trips for €11.35. The hop-on-hop-off bus covers major Gaudí sites efficiently. Walking is the best way to explore the Gothic Quarter and waterfront.

Meal times in Spain are later than most countries: lunch is 2-4 PM, dinner starts at 9-10 PM. Many restaurants close between 4-8 PM. Tipping is not expected but rounding up or leaving €1-2 is common. Siesta culture means some shops close in the afternoon.

Book Sagrada Família tickets at least 2 weeks in advance — it frequently sells out. Park Güell requires timed entry tickets. Casa Batlló and La Pedrera also benefit from advance booking.

Late spring (May-June) and early fall (September-October) are ideal. August is hot and many locals leave the city. Beach season runs June through September.

Tapas at local bars cost €3-8 per plate. The Boqueria market on Las Ramblas has good food stalls but tourist-inflated prices — try Mercat de Sant Antoni instead. A menú del día (set lunch) at local restaurants runs €10-15 for multiple courses.
